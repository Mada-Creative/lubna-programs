# 07 — Page-Type Playbooks

> These rules are mandatory when relevant. They are not visual templates. The business, audience, content, and page goal always come first.

Each playbook below is a narrative starting point for a category of business or page — it tells you what the story usually needs to prove and in what order. It is **not** a visual template and does not override the mandatory process in [01-apple-design-foundations.md](01-apple-design-foundations.md): understand the business, audience, and page goal before applying any playbook, and adapt or discard parts of it as the specific project demands. Every playbook is still bound by [05-anti-ai-design.md](05-anti-ai-design.md).

## Landing Pages

- Single product or single offer, single narrative arc, single primary CTA repeated at most two or three times as the story progresses.
- No unrelated tangents — every section supports the one conversion goal.

## Corporate / Service Sites

- Lead with clarity about who they are and who they serve, not a vague mission statement. Trust is built through specificity: real services, real process, real credentials.
- Human-first tone; avoid corporate-generic stock language (see anti-AI copy rules in [05-anti-ai-design.md](05-anti-ai-design.md)).

## Architecture Studios

- Portfolio work is the argument — minimize text, maximize full-bleed, high-quality imagery with generous whitespace between projects.
- Let composition itself (asymmetry, scale, controlled width — see [03-typography-layout-spacing.md](03-typography-layout-spacing.md)) reflect the studio's design sensibility; the site is itself a portfolio piece.

## Portfolios (Individual)

- Work-first, personality-second: the strongest work leads, personal voice comes through in writing tone rather than decorative flourishes.
- Keep navigation and chrome minimal so the work has no competition for attention.

## Product Marketing

- Feature-to-benefit narrative: every feature shown must connect immediately to a real benefit, demonstrated visually, not just claimed in text.
- Visual proof (product shots, real UI, real demos) over illustrative/abstract graphics.

## Ecommerce

- Conversion clarity above all: clear pricing, clear product state (in stock, sizes, variants), minimal-friction path to purchase.
- Product photography discipline — consistent lighting, angle, and background across the catalog. Trust signals (reviews, shipping/return policy) must be real, never fabricated (see [05-anti-ai-design.md](05-anti-ai-design.md)).

## SaaS

- Explicitly avoid the generic SaaS template banned in [05-anti-ai-design.md](05-anti-ai-design.md). Differentiate through the product's actual workflow: show the real interface solving a real problem, not abstract feature icons.
- Pricing, when present, should be presented as plainly and honestly as the rest of the narrative — no manufactured urgency or fake "most popular" badges unless genuinely true.

## Clinics / Healthcare

- Calm, trustworthy tone; credentials and qualifications stated plainly and verifiably.
- Accessibility is especially critical here (older or anxious visitors, medical decisions) — treat the accessibility rules in [01-apple-design-foundations.md](01-apple-design-foundations.md) and [08-design-audit-quality-gate.md](08-design-audit-quality-gate.md) as strict minimums, not aspirational.

## Restaurants

- Sensory, imagery-led storytelling: food, atmosphere, and craft carry the narrative more than copy does.
- Practical information (menu, hours, location, reservation) must remain easy to find despite the atmospheric visual focus.

## Hospitality

- Experience narrative: the story is the feeling of being there — place, atmosphere, service — told primarily through strong photography/video and restrained, evocative copy.
- Booking/inquiry action should be easy to reach without breaking the atmosphere with an aggressive CTA.

## Real Estate

- The property (or portfolio of properties) is the hero; spatial storytelling through photography, floor plans, and location context.
- Map/location integration should be functional and unobtrusive, not a decorative embed.

## Luxury

- Restraint itself is the luxury signal — generous whitespace, few words, exceptional imagery quality, quiet confidence.
- Explicitly reject the "fake premium" formula (gold accents + black background + serif by default) called out in [05-anti-ai-design.md](05-anti-ai-design.md). Luxury is demonstrated through craft and specificity, not a color palette.

## Courses / Education

- Outcome-first narrative: lead with what the learner will be able to do, not a generic curriculum list.
- Instructor/institution credibility established with real, specific proof; student outcomes shown honestly.

## Events

- Clear, scannable schedule/logistics information; a single primary CTA (register/attend).
- Urgency (countdown, limited seats) is only used when genuinely true — fabricated scarcity violates the anti-AI rules in [05-anti-ai-design.md](05-anti-ai-design.md).

## Nonprofits

- Mission narrative first, told through real impact and real people, not generic charity stock imagery or invented statistics.
- Donation/action path must be clear and low-friction without overwhelming the mission story with commercial-style CTA pressure.

## Service Pages

- Each service page tells the story of that specific service (problem it solves, how it's delivered, what the outcome looks like) — not a generic reworded feature list shared across every service page on the site.
- Avoid identical structure copy-pasted across every service page; vary emphasis based on what actually differentiates each service.

## About Pages

- Real story, real people, real values — specific to this organization's actual history and mission, not generic "who we are" boilerplate.
- Team presentation should feel intentional (consistent photography, meaningful bios), not a templated grid of headshot-name-title cards with no further context.

## Contact Pages

- Clarity and minimal friction over cleverness — the visitor's goal is to reach the business, not admire a contact page.
- Provide the specific contact paths that actually make sense for this business (form, phone, address, map, hours) rather than a generic all-of-the-above pattern.

## Case Studies

- Problem → approach → solution → real, specific result. Results should be concrete and honest; if quantified outcomes aren't available, describe the qualitative outcome specifically rather than inventing a number (see [05-anti-ai-design.md](05-anti-ai-design.md)).
- Show real process artifacts where possible (real screens, real work) rather than generic illustrative graphics standing in for the actual project.
